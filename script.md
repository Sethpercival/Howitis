## Narration (exact, captions match word for word)

Your fridge never makes cold. It moves heat. It pulls heat out of your food, carries it around a loop, and dumps it into your kitchen. Here is how that loop works.

Everything runs on one fluid, called refrigerant. It boils at a freezing temperature, so it can grab heat even inside a cold fridge.

Step one. The compressor. This pump squeezes the refrigerant gas into a tight space. Squeezing raises its pressure, and its temperature shoots up. Now it is a hot, high pressure gas.

Step two. The condenser. The hot gas flows through coils on the back of the fridge. Heat escapes into the room, the gas cools down, and it turns into a liquid.

Step three. The expansion valve. The liquid pushes through a tiny opening. Its pressure drops all at once, and it gets extremely cold, well below freezing.

Step four. The evaporator. The cold liquid flows through coils inside the fridge. It absorbs heat from your food, and boils back into a gas. That is the heat leaving your food.

Then the gas returns to the compressor, and the loop starts again, hundreds of times a day.

So remember the loop. Squeeze it. Shed the heat. Expand it. Absorb the heat. Your fridge is not a cold maker. It is a heat mover.
